Interviewer: Thank you for participating in our research study on solo female travel safety. Can you tell me a bit about your background and your recent solo trip?
Participant P01: I am a 23-year-old postgraduate student from Germany studying in the UK. Last summer, I decided to do a two-week solo backpacking trip through Northern Spain and Italy. It was my very first time travelling entirely by myself without family or friends.

Interviewer: How did your family react when you told them you were planning to travel alone?
Participant P01: Oh, my parents were extremely worried. My mother freaked out and said, "Are you crazy? A young girl travelling alone with a backpack is asking for trouble." They kept sending me sensational news articles about women being harassed abroad. That created a lot of familial anxiety and second-guessing in my mind before I even booked the flight. I felt like society constantly reminds women that public space is risky for us.

Interviewer: During the trip itself, what were your main safety concerns, and did you experience any moments where you felt vulnerable or uncomfortable?
Participant P01: My biggest fear was always after sunset and dealing with night transport. In Rome, when I was walking back from the train station to my hostel around 10 PM, a man started following me down a dimly lit street. He was whistling and catcalling, and then started walking at the exact same pace as me. I felt terrified and my heart was pounding. The public infrastructure was really poor—there was barely any street lighting and no CCTV or people around. I ducked quickly into an open gelato shop and pretended to order until he walked away.

Interviewer: That sounds frightening. What specific strategies or coping mechanisms did you use to protect yourself while travelling?
Participant P01: I developed a strict daily routine. First, I always made sure to return to my accommodation before 9 PM so I wouldn't have to navigate dark alleys alone. Second, I wore fake headphones without playing any music—that way men wouldn't bother trying to talk to me, but I maintained full situational awareness of my surroundings. I also kept my phone's WhatsApp live location permanently shared with my sister back home, and I always booked female-only dorm rooms with secure electronic door locks. Trusting my gut feeling was key—if a street or a person felt off, I immediately crossed the road or left.

Interviewer: How did these safety considerations influence your overall travel decisions and your perception of the destination?
Participant P01: It definitely restricted my itinerary. There were picturesque neighborhoods in Naples and Barcelona that I skipped completely because online forums and reviews warned about street crime and aggressive catcalling. So my perceived safety directly shaped where I went and how freely I moved.

Interviewer: Looking back at the trip, how did navigating these safety constraints affect your personal confidence and well-being?
Participant P01: Despite the moments of fear, finishing the trip was the most liberating and empowering experience of my life. I proved to myself and my family that I am capable, independent, and resilient. Handling those tricky situations gave me immense self-leadership and confidence that carried over into my academic studies. I am already planning my next solo journey.
