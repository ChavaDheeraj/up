Interviewer: Welcome and thank you for taking the time to speak with us. Could you introduce yourself and tell us about where you travelled solo?
Participant P02: Hello! I'm 25, an international graduate student from Singapore. Over the winter break, I took a solo month-long trip across Rajasthan and Himachal Pradesh in India, followed by northern Thailand.

Interviewer: What social or cultural constraints did you encounter regarding travelling alone as a woman?
Participant P02: In many small towns in India, the patriarchal gaze and cultural norms were very noticeable. Public squares and street tea stalls were almost 100% male-dominated spaces. I felt like an alien being stared at incessantly. People would ask me intrusive personal questions: "Where is your husband? Why are your parents allowing you to wander alone?" There is a heavy cultural expectation that an unaccompanied woman is abnormal or vulnerable. I felt like I had to strictly police my clothing, wearing loose kurtas and shawls covering my shoulders and chest just to minimize unwanted stares.

Interviewer: Were there physical safety risks or infrastructure problems that impacted you?
Participant P02: Yes, transit safety was a constant cognitive load. Booking sleeper trains and night buses made me very anxious because of stories of harassment and theft. In one overnight bus journey, a male passenger kept trying to strike uncomfortably intimate conversation and shifted towards my seat. Also, finding clean, secure public toilets with working locks along highways was a major challenge. In hotels, I would always check that the window latches worked and wedged a rubber doorstop under my door before sleeping.

Interviewer: How did you negotiate these constraints? What tactics helped you feel in control?
Participant P02: I wore a cheap fake wedding ring on my left hand and would casually mention my fictitious "husband who is a military officer meeting me at the hotel." That immediately shuts down persistent men. For navigation, I downloaded offline Google Maps and always arrived in new cities before sunset. I also made friends with local families and other solo female travellers in homestays. There is a strong informal sisterhood among women travellers on the road where we share real-time advice on safe drivers and safe neighborhoods.

Interviewer: How did this impact your destination image and overall well-being?
Participant P02: My destination image became nuanced—I fell in love with the cultural warmth and kindness of ordinary people, but I was constantly exhausted by the vigilance required. However, successfully navigating a complex country solo transformed my sense of agency. I learned how to stand tall, look assertive, and say a firm "No" without smiling. It elevated my self-reliance and psychological resilience tremendously.
