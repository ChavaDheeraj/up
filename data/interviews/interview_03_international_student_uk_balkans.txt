Interviewer: Thank you for joining us today. Could you briefly introduce yourself and share the destination of your solo trip?
Participant P03: Sure! I am 24 years old, an international student from Colombia currently pursuing my Master's degree in London. Last autumn, I decided to travel solo through Albania, Montenegro, and Bosnia for three weeks.

Interviewer: What motivated you to choose those destinations, and what safety concerns crossed your mind beforehand?
Participant P03: I wanted an affordable, culturally rich adventure away from standard tourist hotspots. But before leaving, my friends and parents cautioned me repeatedly: "Isn't the Balkan region still unstable? Is it safe for an unaccompanied Latin woman?" The media depiction and lack of mainstream information created a high perceived risk in my mind. I was primarily worried about language barriers, hitchhiking culture, and finding reliable taxis or buses in mountainous areas where infrastructure is rudimentary.

Interviewer: How did you navigate those physical and logistical constraints on the ground?
Participant P03: I had a clear protocol. I bought an eSIM immediately upon landing so I was never disconnected from cellular data. Whenever I took long-distance intercity minibuses (furgons), I deliberately chose a seat right behind the driver or next to elderly women or mothers with children. I never consumed alcohol alone in bars, and I kept copies of my passport in my daypack and another in my locker. More than anything, situational awareness and respecting local cultural customs—like dressing conservatively in traditional rural towns—prevented any unwanted male aggression.

Interviewer: What were the most significant psychological takeaways from managing these risks independently?
Participant P03: There was a dramatic shift in my self-leadership. Before this trip, I was someone who relied heavily on friends to make decisions. When you're solo and a bus doesn't show up in a remote town, panic gets you nowhere; you have to stay calm, trust your instincts, and problem-solve. When I returned to London, I felt invincible. Solo travel completely redefined my sense of autonomy, self-reliance, and mental resilience.
